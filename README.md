# Ofortuna Overview

This app should be a business formation and compliance concierge platform for Jamaica first, then the wider Caribbean. The underlying legal material covers the most common procedures around choosing a business vehicle, forming companies, conducting meetings, changing company names/articles, appointing or removing directors, dissolving companies, restoring struck-off companies, registering overseas companies, forming partnerships, drafting partnership agreements, and winding up partnerships. It also spans Jamaica, Belize, St. Kitts and Nevis, and Antigua and Barbuda.

The strongest version of the app is not “read law notes online.” It should be a guided workflow tool + service layer that turns legal procedure into clear action steps. That matters because the manual itself says it is an aid, not a substitute for primary law, and that it is provided for student use only. So the business should use the material as internal training and process inspiration, while the app itself should rely on your own original workflows, checklists, client intake, service logic, and current registry/legal requirements.

What the app would do

At a high level, the app would help a person go from:

“I want to start or fix a business”
to
“Here is the right structure, here are the documents, here are the next steps, here is the filing path, and here is what I must keep up with after registration.”

So the platform would do five main jobs:

1. Entity selection
The user answers guided questions and the app helps narrow whether they likely need a sole proprietorship, partnership, private company limited by shares, company limited by guarantee, or another structure. That matches the source material’s focus on advising on the appropriate legal vehicle for a venture.

2. Formation workflow
Once the structure is chosen, the app turns the legal procedure into a checklist: what information is needed, what documents are required, what approvals are needed, and what must be filed. The materials specifically focus on formation and operationalization of companies and partnerships.

3. Document and information collection
The app gathers client info in the right sequence: owners, directors, address, share structure, business activity, signing persons, beneficial ownership details, partnership terms, and so on. The materials repeatedly treat legal work here as gathering detailed client information, preparing documents, and managing required filings.

4. Ongoing compliance tracking
After setup, the app should track recurring obligations like annual returns, beneficial ownership updates, director changes, company meetings, changes to articles, name changes, and other routine company housekeeping. The materials make clear that company law practice is not just formation; it also includes operational procedures, meetings, director changes, dissolution, restoration, and ongoing obligations for foreign companies.

5. Escalation to a human service
When the matter becomes complex, the app should route the file to a lawyer or trained service operator. That fits the source material well, because the content is very procedural and practice-oriented, but still tied to statute and legal judgment.

What normal users would experience

For a normal user, this should feel like:

“I answer a few questions, upload my ID and business details, and the platform tells me what to do next without me having to decode legal language.”

Their journey would look like this:

Choose what they want to do: start a business, register a company, create a partnership agreement, fix overdue filings, restore a struck-off company, register an overseas company, or dissolve a business.

Fill out a smart intake form.

Get a recommended path and plain-English explanation.

See a clear list of documents and fees.

Upload what is needed.

Approve drafts or summaries.

Track progress.

Get reminders after registration.

That saves normal users time because they do not have to read statutes, search scattered registry information, guess which form applies, or repeatedly go back and forth with a lawyer just to answer basic intake questions. The course materials themselves are built around technical, common procedures that new practitioners repeatedly handle, which is exactly the kind of work a guided intake-and-workflow system can simplify.

What lawyers would experience

For lawyers, the app should feel like a matter-intake, triage, drafting, and compliance operations tool.

Instead of starting every file from zero, the lawyer gets:

a completed structured intake

a client summary

flagged issues

a recommended entity path

a document checklist

first-draft resolutions, letters, and filing packs

a deadline/compliance calendar

a clean matter dashboard

That saves lawyers time because the materials show that much of this work is repetitive practice and procedure: choosing the vehicle, formation, meetings, changes, director matters, dissolution, restoration, and partnership arrangements. Those are exactly the kinds of matters where lawyers lose time to admin, client chasing, and redoing standard documents.

So for lawyers, the app is not replacing legal judgment. It is removing the low-value repetition around:

initial client education

intake calls

missing-information follow-up

first-draft admin docs

deadline tracking

compliance reminders

matter status updates

How it saves time for both sides

For normal users, time is saved by reducing confusion.

Instead of:

“Should I be a company or a partnership?”

“What documents do I need?”

“What happens after registration?”

“What if I need to change directors or restore a struck-off company?”

They get one guided path. The materials are broad enough to support exactly these common issues.

For lawyers, time is saved by reducing repetitive operations.

Instead of spending the first half of every file:

teaching the basics

collecting scattered facts

sending the same checklists

rebuilding standard docs

reminding clients about routine obligations

the lawyer starts with a cleaner, more complete file. Because the source material is heavily focused on common procedural work, it is ideal for standardization into workflows.

Best positioning

I would position it like this:

For users:
“Start, structure, and maintain your business properly.”

For lawyers:
“A Caribbean business-formation workflow engine that cuts admin and speeds up routine corporate work.”

Best business shape

Since you corrected it to a service business, the best model is probably:

Front end: app for intake, education, tracking, reminders
Back end: human service team and lawyer network

So the app is the engine, but the business sells:

formation support

compliance support

document prep

lawyer review

ongoing company secretarial-style help

That is stronger than a pure software play because the underlying work is procedural but still often needs judgment, especially once the matter moves beyond a basic filing. The source material itself treats this area as technical, practice-based, and statute-led.

The simplest way to say what the app does is:

It turns Caribbean business registration and compliance from a confusing legal process into a guided, trackable service workflow for founders and lawyers.
